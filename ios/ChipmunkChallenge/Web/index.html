Rules the engine enforces

  1. The board is 40×40. Ants reach a block from any side: a block is edible when any face touches open space, meaning the board's outer edge or an empty cell connected to it. Enclosed holes, like the donut's, stay sealed until the ring around them breaks.
  2. Tapping a front nest moves it into a free holding slot below the board; its ants walk out of that slot, across the apron and up into the picture. The holding slot is the nest's mound. even while other nests are still working. Each nest in a slot sends out one ant every 0.32 s, so ants of one nest leave 2 body lengths apart and several nests work side by side.
  3. An ant reserves one edible block of its color as it leaves: the one nearest the bottom center of the board, measured as horizontal distance plus 3 × vertical distance, so a wide stretch of each row clears before the row above. The block stays solid until that ant reaches it: no other ant can take it, ants cannot walk through it, and the blocks behind it stay sealed. The space opens only when the ant picks the block up.
  4. Every ant walks at 16.9 cells per second at 1×, so far blocks take longer. After the pickup it carries the block back to the drain at the bottom.
  5. A nest can have at most 15 ants out at once. When 15 are out, its next ant leaves only after one gets back to the drain.
  6. Ants move in straight, right-angle lines and prefer cover. A step beside the frame or a block costs 1, a step across open ground costs 4, and each ant takes the cheapest route even when it's longer. Ants run along the frame and the edges of the picture and cross open areas by the shortest gap. Among equally cheap routes they avoid turning.
  7. Ants carry each block back to the drain in the middle of the apron. A nest leaves its slot when its last ant departs. When every slot is full, tapping a nest queues it to load the moment a slot opens. Up to 9 can wait, numbered in order. A nest further back can be queued once every nest in front of it in that column is queued. Double-tapping a queued nest cancels just that nest, and the rest move up. A queued nest that still has an unqueued nest ahead of it in its column waits for that one to load, while the queue carries on with the next nest that's ready. A nest with nothing edible waits and keeps its slot. You lose when every slot is held by a waiting nest and every ant is home. You win when the board is empty. Hearts: you have up to 5. Failing or restarting a board costs one (never more than one per attempt); clearing a board, or every 10 minutes of play, gives one back. At 0 hearts the board locks and the countdown runs on the real clock, or you can refill all 5 hearts for all of your coins. Clearing a board pays 50 coins on Easy, 100 on Normal and 200 on Hard, plus 50% if you cleared it on the first try (no restart, no undo, no auto-solve).
  8. The game runs on a fixed 10 ms clock, and the solver runs that exact same simulation, so its verdicts match play. It assumes you tap only once every ant is home. Tapping earlier is allowed; the hint then replans from wherever the ants actually are.

How levels are built

  1. Each scene fills the whole 40×40 frame. Subjects carry a black outline, and backgrounds are checkerboards, wood grain, stripes or three-color mosaics. Most colors start buried and come in small patches, so a nest often finds only part of its color in reach.
  2. Any photo can also become a board with the + Image button: center-cropped, averaged into 40×40 cells, snapped to the standard colors, cleaned of stray pixels, and colors with fewer than 15 blocks folded into their nearest neighbor.
  3. buildSequence plays the picture forward on the timed simulation, opening nests of colors that have an edible block, sized within the challenge's range and never leaving a leftover under 5 ants. Tapping in that order always clears the board.
  4. Any deal of those nests into lanes that keeps their order inside each lane stays solvable, so the generator searches over deals. tuneLanes scores each deal with a random-tapper model (the one behind the 💪 score) and keeps changes that move its win rate toward the challenge target. A careful-player model, which taps exposed colors first and prefers nests that can empty and free their slot, is also measured for the workbench.
  5. Scoring games rotate every 15 steps so the deal can't overfit them, and the finished board is re-scored on fresh games. That fresh number is the one shown above. Targets: Easy 70%, Normal 30%, Hard 10% of random-tap games won, so 💪 about 1.4, 3.3 and 10. Some pictures can't reach the harder targets: their colors open up in an order that leaves few ways to go wrong.
  6. solve first replays the stored solution from wherever you are. If that no longer clears the board, it falls back to a depth-first search. Hints, auto-solve and the lane checks run in a background worker so the game never stalls.
